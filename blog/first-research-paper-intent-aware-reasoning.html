Our First Research Paper Is Coming in December 2026

CoreMind Labs is preparing its first paper, on intent-aware reasoning in large language models. Here is the question it explores, and why it matters.

CL
CoreMind Labs, Fortress Technologies Hub
November 2, 2026 · 2 min read

Ask an AI assistant to "make this shorter," and it will usually do exactly that. It cuts words. Sometimes it cuts the part that mattered most. The request was followed, but the goal was missed.

That gap between what someone says and what they mean is the focus of the first paper from CoreMind Labs, the research group at Fortress Technologies Hub. The paper, Intent-Aware Reasoning in Large Language Models, is expected in December 2026.

The question

Language models are very good at following instructions literally. But people rarely give complete instructions. Requests are short, ambiguous, and missing context, because we expect the listener to fill in the gaps the way another person would.

The paper explores how a model can reason about the intent behind a request before it answers, by considering:

  • Purpose: what is this person actually trying to achieve?
  • Audience: who is the output for?
  • Context: what does the surrounding conversation or document reveal?

Back to "make this shorter." A model that reasons about intent asks why. Is this a summary for an executive? A post with a character limit? A paragraph full of filler? The answer changes what a good result looks like: concise, complete, and still faithful to the original meaning.

Why it matters

Intent is not only a convenience problem. It is an alignment problem. A system that understands what people mean is more useful, needs fewer retries, and is less likely to produce something technically correct but wrong for the person's real goal. We believe safety and capability are not in conflict, and this research is part of how we intend to show it.

How it connects to CoreMind

This work shapes how CoreMind's models approach requests. It is also why reasoning mode shows its work: when you can see how a model interpreted your request, you can correct it before it goes wrong.

How we do research

  • Open publication. We intend to share our findings with the research community, including results that challenge our own assumptions.
  • Transparent reasoning. We favor systems that show their work, because reasoning you can verify builds trust.
  • Built in Canada. Our research infrastructure, model training, and core models run on Canadian-owned infrastructure in Canada.
